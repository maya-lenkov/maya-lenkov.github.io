Project 04 / Digitas

Rugby · From audience research to recommendations

I built a Rugby World Cup audience persona analysis using consumer, behavioral, demographic, and financial information. I compared broad rugby interest with audiences narrowed by live-event attendance and geography, then translated those differences into recommendations.

What I found

  • Broad interest was better suited to awareness. The general-interest audience offered reach, but did not justify assuming that every rugby fan was affluent or ready to buy.
  • Event attendance sharpened the commercial audience. The event-attendee segment showed a more financially established profile and stronger signals relevant to tickets, hospitality, and merchandise.
  • Rugby interest overlapped with other sports. Cross-sport interests suggested opportunities to connect rugby messaging with other live sporting moments.
  • Strong affinity did not always mean broad reach. Streaming and niche interests offered targeted opportunities, but were not characteristics to generalize to the whole audience.
  • Geography changed the activation opportunity. Host-market audience cuts supported local activation planning, while regional data required care before drawing city-level conclusions.

How the findings informed my recommendations

I recommended using the broad audience for awareness, the event-attendee segment for revenue-focused activity, and geographic cuts for local activation. I treated niche interests as targeted tests rather than the foundation of the entire plan.

The analysis reinforced the importance of reading relative affinity alongside audience share: a highly concentrated signal can still describe only a small part of an audience.

Qualitative findings only. Source data, exact statistics, audience counts, index values, and market rankings are not published.
